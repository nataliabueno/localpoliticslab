Political Science · Emory University

The politics of local governments and public policy.

The Local Politics and Policy Lab studies how local governments, public policies, and elections shape political behavior and democratic accountability — with a focus on Brazil and Latin America.

We combine field and survey experiments, original administrative data, and causal-inference methods to understand distributive politics, housing and welfare, misinformation, and political representation. The lab is directed by Natália S. Bueno, Associate Professor of Political Science at Emory University.

Explore our research Meet the lab

What we study

We ask how the everyday machinery of local government — who gets what, when, and how they know it — shapes citizens’ politics.

Local & distributive

How governments target benefits, claim credit, and delegate services — and how voters respond.

Multi-method

Experiments, original administrative and survey data, and formal design for credible causal inference.

Global South focus

Deep empirical grounding in Brazil and Latin America, with implications for democracies everywhere.

Open & reproducible

Committed to transparency, replicability, and tools that make research more credible.

Research

Five threads of local politics

Our work clusters around how local institutions distribute resources and information, and how those choices reshape elections, accountability, and representation.

01

Distributive Politics & Credit Claiming

How governments time, target, and take credit for the benefits they deliver — and how those strategies convert policy into political support.

  • The Timing of Public Policies: Political Budget Cycles and Credit Claiming PDF Bueno. American Journal of Political Science, 2021.
  • Bypassing the Enemy: Distributive Politics, Credit Claiming, and Non-State Organizations in Brazil PDF Bueno. Comparative Political Studies, 2018.
  • What You See and What You Get: The Direct and Indirect Political Dividends of Public Policies PDF Bueno, Nunes & Zucco. British Journal of Political Science, 2023.
02

Housing & Social Policy

How housing and welfare programs reshape political behavior, social cohesion, and well-being — and how the way benefits are allocated changes their effects.

  • Making the Bourgeoisie? Values, Voice, and State-Provided Home Ownership Bueno, Zucco & Nunes. Journal of Politics, 2022.
  • Benefits by Luck: A Study of Lotteries as a Selection Method for Government Programs PDF Bueno, Nunes & Zucco. Comparative Political Studies, 2024.
  • Housing, Social Cohesion, and Well-Being: Experimental Evidence from Informal Settlements Bueno, Avelino, Biderman, Bueno & Da Mata. Revised and resubmitted.
03

Elections & Misinformation

How false information spreads through elections in the Global South — and what actually works to blunt it, from inoculation to fact-checking.

  • The Liar’s Dividend: Can Politicians Claim Misinformation to Evade Accountability? PDF Bueno, Schiff & Schiff. American Political Science Review, 2024.
  • Inoculation Reduces Misinformation: Experimental Evidence from a Multidimensional Intervention in Brazil PDF Bueno, Batista Pereira, Nunes & Pavão. Journal of Experimental Political Science, 2024. APSA Best Research Article Award
  • How to Fight Election Misinformation PDF Bueno. Science Advances, 2025.
  • Fake News, Fact Checking, and Partisanship: The Resilience of Rumors in the 2018 Brazilian Elections PDF Bueno, Batista Pereira, Nunes & Pavão. Journal of Politics, 2022.
04

Accountability & Federalism

How partisan favoritism persists across levels of government, and why accountability institutions do not always constrain it.

  • Coordination Advantage: How Partisan Favoritism Persists Under Accountability Institutions Meireles & Bueno. Under review. 2026 Deil S. Wright Best Paper Award
05

Race, Resources & Representation

How race shapes political participation and representation, using original evidence from Brazilian voters and politicians.

  • Race, Resources, and Representation: Evidence from Brazilian Politicians PDF Bueno & Dunning. World Politics, 2017.
  • Race, Resources, and Political Participation in a Brazilian City Bueno & Fialho. Latin American Research Review, 2009.

People

The lab

Natália S. Bueno

Director · Associate Professor of Political Science, Emory University

Natália studies comparative politics and public policy, with an emphasis on how local governments distribute resources and information and how those choices shape elections, political behavior, and accountability. Her work draws on experiments, original administrative data, and causal-inference methods, primarily in Brazil and Latin America. She received her Ph.D. from Yale University; her research has appeared in the American Journal of Political Science, American Political Science Review, the Journal of Politics, World Politics, and Comparative Political Studies, among others.

She is a recipient of the APSA Urban and Local Politics Section’s Clarence Stone Scholar Award (2024) and the Harold D. Lasswell Award for best dissertation in public policy (2019).

Clara Bicalho

Postdoctoral Associate

Clara holds a Ph.D. in political science from UC Berkeley. Her research examines the determinants of collective land-titling reforms in Latin America and their effects on identity, governance, and political participation — with fieldwork on quilombo communities in Brazil — and she builds tools for causal inference, reproducibility, and research transparency.
